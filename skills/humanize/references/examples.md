# Before and after

Five short examples showing what a good edit looks like. Use them to calibrate how much to change. Each "after" keeps every fact from the "before" and adds none. Where a fact is missing, the edit flags it instead of inventing it.

---

## 1. LinkedIn post (light edit)

**Before**

> Here's the thing nobody tells you about dashboards. It's not a tools problem. It's a trust problem. When finance stopped double-checking our numbers, meetings got 20 minutes shorter. Let that sink in.

**After**

> Our dashboards had a trust problem. When finance stopped double-checking our numbers, meetings got 20 minutes shorter.

**What changed**

- A1 secret-insight framing: cut "Here's the thing nobody tells you".
- B1 binary reframe: "It's not X. It's Y." became one direct claim.
- C3 reaction narration: cut "Let that sink in". The 20 minutes makes the point.

---

## 2. Business email (light edit)

**Before**

> I hope this email finds you well! I just wanted to circle back on the proposal. It's worth noting that we're keen to move forward. Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions.

**After**

> Are you ready to sign off on the proposal? We'd like to move forward, so let me know if anything is holding it up.

**What changed**

- Email genre: the ask moved to the first sentence.
- D2 filler: cut "I hope this email finds you well", "just wanted to circle back", "It's worth noting".
- Closing: "Please don't hesitate to reach out" became a specific question.

---

## 3. Website copy (light edit, with a flag)

**Before**

> No setup. No code. Just insights. Our cutting-edge platform empowers teams to unlock the full potential of their data.

**After**

> Connect your data and get reports without writing code or setting anything up. [Add one concrete result, such as how long the first report takes.]

**What changed**

- B2 negative stack: "No setup. No code. Just insights." became one sentence.
- C6 sales language and D1 stock words: cut "cutting-edge", "empowers", "unlock the full potential".
- Flagged: the draft has no specific outcome. The edit asks for one instead of making it up.

---

## 4. Report paragraph (light edit, with a flag)

**Before**

> Studies show that automation may potentially reduce errors. The new process was rolled out in Q3, highlighting the team's commitment to quality.

**After**

> Automation may reduce errors [source needed: which study, and what did it find?]. The team rolled out the new process in Q3.

**What changed**

- C4 vague authority: "Studies show" flagged for a real source.
- C5 hedge stacking: "may potentially" became "may".
- C2 hollow analysis: cut ", highlighting the team's commitment to quality". Passive "was rolled out" became active.

---

## 5. Launch post (rewrite)

**Before**

> I built Ledgerly, an open-source tool for small finance teams that automates bank reconciliation and flags mismatches, and most tools in this space are expensive and hard to set up.

**After**

> I couldn't find a reconciliation tool that was affordable and easy to set up, so I built Ledgerly. It matches bank transactions automatically and flags anything that doesn't line up. It's open source and made for small finance teams.

**What changed**

- A6 definition opener: the post now opens with why it exists.
- C7 straw competitor: "most tools in this space are expensive" became the writer's own reason. No new backstory was added.
- B10 stuffed sentence: one sentence with four ideas became three short ones.
- Spoken verbs: "automates bank reconciliation" became "matches bank transactions automatically".
